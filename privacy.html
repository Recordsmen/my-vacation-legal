Privacy Policy

My Vacation · Last updated: 4 November 2026

The short version

  • My Vacation works on your phone without an account. Your photos stay on the phone unless you use an online feature.
  • Photos in Cloud Backup, share links and group trips are end-to-end encrypted: we store files we can't open. We see a photo only if someone reports it and chooses to send us a copy or the link's key, or if you choose an unencrypted chat preview for a link.
  • No ads, no advertising IDs, no selling or sharing of personal data, no tracking across other companies' apps or websites.
  • We collect usage statistics that aren't linked to your account (you can turn them off) and crash reports, through Google Firebase.
  • You can export or delete your online data in Settings → Manage account.

This policy explains what My Vacation (the app, the share-link web pages and our server) collects, why, how long we keep it, who processes it and what you can do about it. The regional sections at the end add what the law of your country requires.

1. Who is responsible

My Vacation is made by Oleksandr Uchytelev, an individual developer based in Israel ("we", "us"). We decide how your personal data is used (in EU terms, we are the controller).

  • Email for privacy questions and requests: ceoolik@gmail.com

The same person acts as privacy officer, data protection contact and grievance officer under the laws listed in the regional sections.

2. On your phone

Photos you take or pick, vacation names, dates and reminder times are stored in the app's private storage on your device. Unless you use one of the online features below, none of it leaves the phone and we cannot see it. Deleting the app deletes it.

On iPhone, the app's data is part of your iCloud or computer backup, under Apple's terms. On Android it is left out of the Google cloud backup (which is too small for a vacation of photos) and moves to a new phone only with a direct phone-to-phone transfer. Photos sent to a share link or a group trip are re-encoded first, which removes their embedded metadata, including any location (GPS in EXIF).

3. Camera, photos and notifications

  • Camera: to take your morning, day and evening photos inside the app.
  • Picking from the gallery: uses the system photo picker, so the app only receives the photo you choose. It reads that photo's capture date to check it was taken during the current slot.
  • Saving to Photos: on iPhone the app asks for add-only access to save a finished collage or film. It never reads your library.
  • Notifications: photo reminders are scheduled on your device. If you use group trips or share links, our server can also send you a push notification (for example "Your group trip is open") through Firebase Cloud Messaging (Google), which uses Apple's push service on iPhone. For that we store your device's push token. Notification texts never contain photos or names.

You can withdraw any of these permissions in the system settings at any time.

4. Your account (only for online features)

The first time you use an online feature (a share link, a group trip or Cloud Backup), the app quietly creates an account. There is no email, phone number, password, or sign-in with Apple or Google. Our server stores:

  • a random account ID and when it was created;
  • for each of your phones: the platform, model and app version, the device's public keys (the private keys never leave the phone), the push token, and when it was last seen;
  • whether the phone passed an app-integrity check: on iPhone, Apple App Attest (we check it on our server and keep the key it creates); on Android, Google Play Integrity (the app's token goes to Google, which tells us whether the app and device look genuine). We keep only "verified" or "limited", not Google's full answer;
  • if you set a recovery key: only its public part, and your encrypted account vault (your group profile and keys), which we cannot read.

Why: to run the features you asked for and to keep the service safe from abuse.

5. Share links

Photos in a share link are encrypted on your phone before upload. The key is the part of the link after the "#", which browsers never send to a server, so we store only encrypted files and cannot see the photos. Anyone you give the link to can open them.

  • What we can see: how many files and how big, when the link was made, its expiry, and view and download counts. By default the trip name and dates are shown in chat previews, so they are stored unencrypted; you can turn that off before sharing. If you pick the collage chat preview, that one picture is uploaded unencrypted so chat apps can show it.
  • Visitors: to count one view per person per day we keep a one-way hash of the visitor's IP address and browser, salted with a value that changes every day, for one day. The link page sets no cookies and loads no analytics or third-party scripts.

6. Group trips

Photos, the trip name and members' names and emoji avatars are encrypted on the phones of the people in the trip; only members can open them. To run the trip our server sees some details unencrypted: the trip's dates and time zone, when it opens, who is a member and their role, and for each photo who took it, the day and slot (morning, day or evening), the size and the upload time. It also keeps votes to open early, blocks, hidden photos and reports.

Group photos stay available to the group so members can download them. When you leave a group you choose whether your photos stay; when you delete your account, your photos are deleted from every group trip.

7. Cloud Backup

Cloud Backup is part of My Vacation+ and is being rolled out. If you turn it on, your trips and photos are encrypted on your phone with a key only you hold (your recovery key) before upload. We store only encrypted files, their sizes and dates, and cannot read them. If you lose your phone and your recovery key, we cannot recover the backup.

8. Reports and moderation

Because shared photos are encrypted, we can only look at one if someone reports it and chooses to let us:

  • on a share link page, the reporter can include the link's key; in a group trip, the reporter can send a copy of the photo (the app asks first). Reports can also include a short note and, if the reporter wants a reply, an email address;
  • a report about a name sends a copy of that name;
  • to stop the same person reporting many times we keep a one-way hash of the reporter's IP address (salted daily), never the address itself;
  • only we, as moderator, can see these copies. How long they are kept is in section 13.

If the content appears to be child sexual abuse material, we report it to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) and, where the law requires, to other authorities, and keep the evidence as the law requires. The rules for shared photos and how decisions and appeals work are in the Terms of Use.

9. Purchases

My Vacation+ is bought through the App Store or Google Play; we never see your name, payment details or billing address. To unlock features on our server (Cloud Backup, group trips), the app sends us the store's purchase proof, which we check with Apple or Google. We keep a one-way hash of the purchase ID, the product, the plan, whether it's a trial, whether it renews, and when it expires. Apple and Google also tell our server when a subscription renews, ends or is refunded.

10. Logs, crash reports and Report a problem

Server logs. Our server keeps short technical logs of each request: the time, the address it called, the result, the platform and the IP address. They contain no photos and no content you write, and help us keep the service working and safe.

Crash reports. If the app crashes, it sends a crash report to Firebase Crashlytics (Google): the device model, OS version, app version, a random installation ID, the technical details of the crash and a short log of what the app did just before it (which screens were opened and which actions ran). Reports contain no photos, vacation names or anything you type, and are not linked to your account. Crash reports can't be turned off in the app; they are how we learn that the app broke on someone's phone.

Report a problem. Settings → Report a problem lets you send us a note; after a crash the app also offers it once on the next launch. Nothing is sent until you press Send. The report contains the text you write, the details of the last crash if you leave "Attach the error" on, and technical diagnostics: app version, OS version, device model, app language, the screen you came from and the app's last 50 log entries (no photos and no vacation content). "Show what's sent" shows all of it before you send. You choose how to send it: through Crashlytics, or by email from your own mail app, in which case we also see your email address and use it only to reply.

Settings. Firebase Remote Config (Google) tells the app which settings and features to use. It uses the same random installation ID and no personal content.

11. Usage statistics

To learn which parts of the app people use and where they get stuck, the app sends usage statistics to Google Analytics for Firebase (Google).

  • What is sent: counts of things that happen in the app, such as "a vacation was created" (with its type, planned length and whether reminders are on), "a photo was sealed" (morning, day or evening; camera or gallery; which day of the trip), "a slot was missed", "a collage was shared" (the kind of image and the app it went to) and which screens were opened. Google adds the device model, OS and app version, language, a coarse region derived from the connection and a random app-instance ID that resets when the app is reinstalled.
  • What is never sent: photos, vacation names, file names or anything you type. The app does not collect the advertising ID (IDFA on iPhone, the Android advertising ID or Android ID), turns off Google's advertising features, does not link statistics to your account and does not track you across other companies' apps or websites.
  • How to turn it off: Settings → Usage statistics. From then on nothing is sent.

12. Processing that never leaves your phone

  • Stickers. To cut a person or object out of a photo, the app uses the phone's built-in image segmentation (Apple Vision on iPhone, Google ML Kit on Android). It finds the outline of the subject in that one photo. It does not recognise who anyone is, does not create a face template, face print or any other biometric identifier, and nothing is sent to us or to Google or Apple.
  • Sensitive-photo warnings. On supported phones, the app can check a photo for nudity before you share it and when others share it with you, and blur it until you tap: on iPhone with Apple's Sensitive Content Analysis when you have turned on Sensitive Content Warnings, on Android with an on-device model as we roll it out. The result stays on the phone. We may receive a count, in the usage statistics, of how often a blur happened or would have happened, never the photo or a score.
  • Trip Film, collages and reveals are made on your phone.

Apart from these providers, we disclose data only: to NCMEC or authorities when we report illegal content; in answer to a valid legal request that binds us; to protect someone's life or safety; or to a successor if the app is ever transferred, who must keep this policy's promises. We do not sell personal data and do not share it for advertising.

15. International transfers

We work from Israel and our providers store data in the United States and other countries, so your data may be processed outside your country. We rely on the safeguards each law provides: for data from the EU, EEA, UK and Switzerland, adequacy decisions (Israel has one, and US companies certified under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and its UK and Swiss extensions) and otherwise the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses (with the UK addendum) in our providers' terms. The regional sections describe other countries' rules. Data that is end-to-end encrypted cannot be read by any provider wherever it is stored.

16. Security

Photos, group names and keys in the online features are encrypted on the phone with keys we don't have (the exceptions you choose are described in sections 5 and 8). Connections use TLS. Device keys sit in the phone's secure hardware (Secure Enclave or Keystore). Access to our systems and to moderation copies is limited to us and logged. If a breach puts your data at risk, we will tell you and the authorities as the law requires.

17. Your choices and rights

  • See and take your data: Settings → Manage account → Export my data gives you a machine-readable copy of what our server stores about your account. Your trips and photos are already on your phone, and you can save them from the app.
  • Delete your data: Settings → Manage account → Delete account removes your account, devices, share links, your photos in group trips and your Cloud Backup from our server straight away. If you no longer have the phone, open the account deletion page and enter your recovery key: it is checked in your browser and never sent to us. What stays after deletion is listed in section 13.
  • Turn things off: Settings → Usage statistics; turn off a share link at any time; leave a group trip.
  • Other rights: depending on where you live, you can also ask us to correct your data, restrict or object to its use, withdraw consent, appeal our answer, or complain to your data-protection authority. See the regional sections.

How to ask. Use the in-app controls, or write to ceoolik@gmail.com. Because accounts are anonymous, we can only link a request to an account through the app on your phone or your recovery key; we never ask for ID documents. We answer within 30 days or sooner where the law requires, and free of charge. You won't be treated differently for using your rights.

We give everyone these rights, whether or not a privacy law applies to us where you live.

18. Children

My Vacation is meant for people aged 13 and over (or the higher age your country sets for using online services without a parent, listed in the regional sections). It is not directed at children and we don't knowingly collect data from children under 13. Younger children may use the app on their own phone without the online features only with a parent. If you think a child has used an online feature without a parent's permission, write to us and we will delete the account.
